# Traditional Machine Learning vs Neural Networks

Not every AI problem requires Deep Learning.

Traditional Machine Learning algorithms are still powerful and are often the best choice depending on the type of data, problem, and available resources.

---

# Traditional Machine Learning Algorithms

Before Neural Networks became popular, Machine Learning was already solving many real-world problems using algorithms such as:

---

## Decision Trees

A Decision Tree works like a flowchart.

It makes decisions by asking a series of questions.

Example:

```
Is income > 50000?
        |
       Yes
        |
Approve Loan
```

Uses:

- Customer classification
- Risk prediction
- Decision systems

---

## Logistic Regression

Used mainly for binary classification problems.

Examples:

```
Spam or Not Spam

Customer will leave or stay

Disease detected or not detected
```

---

## Support Vector Machines (SVM)

SVM finds the best boundary between different categories.

Example:

```
Class A | Boundary | Class B
```

Uses:

- Image classification
- Text classification
- Pattern recognition

---

## K-Nearest Neighbors (KNN)

KNN makes predictions by looking at similar examples nearby.

Example:

If most similar customers bought a product, a new similar customer may also buy it.

Uses:

- Recommendation systems
- Classification tasks

---

## Naive Bayes

A probability-based algorithm that works especially well with text.

Uses:

- Email spam detection
- Sentiment analysis
- Document classification

---

# Neural Networks

## Definition

Neural Networks are Machine Learning models inspired by the structure of the human brain.

They consist of connected layers of artificial neurons that learn complex patterns from data.

Structure:

```
Input Layer
     |
     v
Hidden Layers
     |
     v
Output Layer
```

---

# Why Use Neural Networks?

Neural Networks are powerful when working with:

- Images
- Video
- Audio
- Natural language
- Large datasets

Examples:

- Face recognition
- Speech recognition
- Self-driving cars
- ChatGPT

---

# Limitations of Neural Networks

Although powerful, neural networks have some challenges:

## Data Hungry

They usually require large amounts of training data.

---

## Slow Training

Deep neural networks may require:

- Powerful hardware
- More training time

---

## Harder to Explain

Many neural networks behave like a "black box".

It can be difficult to understand exactly why a model made a decision.

---

# Traditional ML vs Neural Networks

| Factor | Traditional ML | Neural Networks |
|---|---|---|
| Data Size | Works well with smaller datasets | Requires large datasets |
| Interpretability | Easy to understand | Often difficult to explain |
| Training Speed | Faster training | Slower training |
| Data Type | Structured data | Unstructured data |
| Examples | Tables, numbers, categories | Images, audio, text |

---

# Choosing the Right Approach

The best AI solution depends on the problem.

## Example: Banking

### Loan Default Prediction

Input:

- Income
- Credit score
- Employment history

Best choice:

```
Traditional ML
(Logistic Regression / Decision Tree)
```

Reason:

The data is structured and easy to analyze.

---

### Fraud Detection

Input:

- Transactions
- User behavior
- Complex patterns

Best choice:

```
Neural Networks
```

Reason:

The patterns can be complex and difficult to define manually.

---

# Example: Retail

## Sales Forecasting

Data:

- Previous sales
- Prices
- Seasons

Suitable:

```
Traditional ML
```

---

## Product Image Classification

Data:

- Images
- Visual features

Suitable:

```
Neural Networks
```

---

# Key Takeaways

- More advanced does not always mean better.
- Traditional ML is excellent for structured data and smaller datasets.
- Neural Networks are powerful for complex and unstructured data.
- The best AI engineers choose the right tool for the problem.
